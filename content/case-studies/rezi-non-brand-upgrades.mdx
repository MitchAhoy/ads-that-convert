<CaseStudySection title="Context">
Rezi is an AI resume builder used by more than 4 million job seekers. Users sign up free, upload a resume, then upgrade to Pro on a monthly or lifetime plan.

The goal was to grow paid upgrades from people who weren&apos;t already searching for Rezi, at a cost per upgrade below breakeven, as organic search traffic declined.

The catch was that non-brand search barely contributed. In March it drove only about 1 in 10 paid upgrades. Keywords were on broad match, bidding optimised to sign-ups rather than paid upgrades, and mobile took a similar share of spend to desktop while rarely converting.
</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection title="Strategy">
Before adding budget, I fixed what Google was optimising towards, then scaled only while cost per upgrade stayed below breakeven.

- **Bidding to paid upgrades, not sign-ups.** I verified sign-up, resume upload and purchase tracking, then made Pro upgrades the only conversion Google bids towards.
- **Cutting the waste.** Non-brand keywords moved from broad to exact and phrase match, and "free" searches were excluded account-wide. Ads moved to desktop only, where users could sign up and upload a resume in one session, and the click-to-upgrade rate nearly doubled from 4.46% to 8.76%.
- **A clear breakeven line.** I agreed a breakeven cost per upgrade with Rezi up front and only added budget once non-brand converted consistently below it.
- **Competitor pages.** I wrote and wireframed comparison pages against Teal, Jobscan and Kickresume, each with a switching discount, for a dedicated Competitor campaign. In July it was the most efficient non-brand campaign.
- **Ad copy built on paid conversions.** Copy is refreshed quarterly, keeping elements that convert to paid and cutting those that only draw clicks. The top description now runs in every ad, converting at 8.8%.
- **Scaling with guardrails.** Google forecast room to triple spend overnight. Instead, the core Generic campaign&apos;s budget rose 57% over six weeks, with upgrades checked before every increase. Brand and non-brand were reported separately every month so brand efficiency never hid non-brand performance.
</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection title="Results">
September was non-brand&apos;s biggest month since I took over: **129 paid upgrades at 25% below breakeven**, up from 29 in March.

Over April to September, non-brand delivered **687 paid upgrades** and stayed **below breakeven every month**, averaging 20% under. It held there through two new competitors entering the auction and a full website migration.

![Rezi monthly report for non-brand campaigns, April to September 2026: monthly upgrades of 107, 116, 119, 98, 118 and 129, with cost per upgrade at 67%, 75%, 89%, 88%, 86% and 75% of breakeven](/case%20study%20images/results/rezi-monthly-report.png "Monthly report · non-brand, Apr to Sep 2026")

In the first full month, upgrades nearly quadrupled at a similar cost per upgrade. The core Generic campaign then posted its best efficiency to date in September, 28% below breakeven, with upgrades up 14% on August.

Non-brand carried the growth. Its share of Rezi&apos;s paid upgrades rose from about 1 in 10 in March to more than half in September.
</CaseStudySection>
