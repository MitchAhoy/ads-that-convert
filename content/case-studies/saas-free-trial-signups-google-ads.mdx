<CaseStudySection title="Why sign-up volume was the wrong target">
A free sign-up is easy to give away. That makes it a weak signal for Google Ads, because the cheapest sign-ups are often the ones least likely to pay.

This account had both sides of that problem:

1. **A crowded category.** There are hundreds of mass SMS, voice and email tools, so generic searches are competitive and costly.
2. **Sign-ups the business didn't want.** Misuse of SMS and email tools is rampant. Plenty of people sign up with no plan to pay, or to send spam, and a sign-up count can't tell them apart from a real business.

<CaseStudySection title="The free-trial playbook I'd use for your SaaS">
If your Google Ads sends people to a free trial or free sign-up, this is the order I work in:

- **Define a qualified sign-up first.** Agree on one signal that predicts paying: a lead score, a business email, a key action in the product. Do this before touching campaigns.
- **Track three events, not one.** Sign-up, qualified sign-up and paid customer (with its value), each tied to the click with a GCLID stored on the user record.
- **Send qualified and paid events back to Google Ads.** Upload them as offline conversions and make them the conversions Google bids towards, so it learns which clicks become customers. Keep the raw sign-up as a secondary conversion for reporting.
- **Give it enough data, then cut hard.** Wait for enough qualified conversions to judge each keyword, then remove keywords that only drive low-quality sign-ups.
- **Report the funnel monthly.** Sign-ups, qualified sign-ups, paying customers and cost per paying customer, side by side. Total sign-ups on their own will flatter a bad account.
